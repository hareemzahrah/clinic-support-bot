# Loom script — 90 seconds

Record at **https://clinic-support-bot.vercel.app**. Screen only, your voice over it. No webcam
needed, no intro slide, no "hi my name is". A client watching this has already decided to give
you ninety seconds; spend them on the product.

Do a dry run first so you know where you're clicking. Two takes is normal.

---

## 0:00 — Open on the site, chat closed

> "This is a support assistant for a dental practice. It answers patient questions from the
> clinic's own documents — and, more importantly, it knows when it can't."

Open the chat.

## 0:10 — Ask something it can answer

Type: **`how much is a check up`**

Let it stream. Don't talk over the whole answer — let them watch text appear.

> "Fifty-four pounds, seventy-nine for a new patient, twenty-seven ninety on the NHS. Those come
> straight out of the practice's fee guide, and it shows which documents it used."

Click **Sources** to expand the citations.

## 0:25 — The part that matters

> "Now here's the bit that separates this from a chatbot you'd knock together in a weekend."

Type: **`do you offer sedation for nervous patients?`**

> "The practice's documents say nothing about sedation. But they do have a warm paragraph about
> looking after nervous patients — so search returns it, confidently, and it looks relevant.
> That's where most chatbots start making things up."

Let the answer finish.

> "It doesn't. It says it doesn't have that information, tells you what it *does* know, and
> offers to pass the question on."

## 0:50 — Capture the lead

Tick **I'd like an appointment**, fill in a name and a phone number, hit send.

> "A question the website can't answer becomes a name and a number instead of a lost enquiry.
> It doesn't pretend to book — reception rings to confirm."

## 1:05 — The dashboard

Go to **/login**, click **Sign in to the demo**.

> "And the practice gets this. Not conversation counts — a to-do list written by their own
> patients. Six people asked about sedation. Nobody's website mentions it. Half of them asked in
> the evening, when there's no one to answer the phone."

Point at the **Act on this** line, then the gaps table.

## 1:20 — Close

> "Fifty out of fifty answerable questions correct, fifteen out of fifteen unanswerable ones
> refused, zero unsupported claims across the whole suite. Under a penny a message. The code and
> the test suite are on GitHub."

Stop recording.

---

## What not to do

**Don't explain the stack.** Nobody hiring you for a chatbot cares that it's Next.js. If they
care, they'll read the repo.

**Don't apologise for it being a demo.** It's a working, deployed, tested product. Say so.

**Don't run over two minutes.** The sedation refusal is the whole pitch — if you're short on
time, cut the fee question, not that.

**Don't script it word for word.** These are the beats. Say them the way you'd say them to a
person.

- **Upwork profile** — portfolio item, with the live link and the repo
- **Every proposal** where the client wants a chatbot, an AI assistant, or customer support
  automation
- **Pinned to your GitHub profile** via a profile README

## The line to use in proposals

> I built a support assistant for dental practices that answers from the clinic's own documents
> and refuses to invent an answer when they don't cover something — tested against 65 questions,
> zero unsupported claims. Live demo and full source: [links]

Short, specific, and every claim in it is checkable. That last part is what separates it from
every other proposal in the stack.
