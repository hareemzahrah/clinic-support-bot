# New Patients and Emergency Care

## Registering as a new patient

Anyone can register with us as a private patient. We are not currently taking new adult NHS patients — see our NHS and Private Care document for the full position and how to join the expression-of-interest list.

To register, telephone the practice or complete the new patient form on our website. We will ask for your name, date of birth, address, telephone number, email address, your GP's details, and your previous dentist if you had one.

There is no registration fee. Registering does not commit you to anything.

## Your first appointment

Your first appointment is a new patient examination and lasts about 45 minutes. It costs £79 privately.

During it your dentist will:

- Take a full medical history and talk through any concerns you have
- Examine your teeth, gums, bite and the soft tissues of your mouth
- Carry out an oral cancer screening
- Take X-rays if clinically necessary
- Discuss what they have found and set out any treatment you need, with costs, in writing

Routine treatment is not usually carried out at the first appointment, because we want you to have a written plan and time to consider it. The exception is if you are in pain, in which case we will do what we can to make you comfortable that day.

## What to bring

- A list of any medicines you take, including anything bought without a prescription
- Details of any medical conditions and allergies
- Your previous dentist's name and address, if you have one, so we can request your records
- Evidence of NHS exemption, if you are claiming free NHS treatment
- Your dental insurance details or claim form, if you have insurance

Please arrive five minutes early for a first appointment so there is time to complete the medical history form. You can also download it from our website and bring it filled in.

## Transferring your records

With your written consent we will request your records and recent X-rays from your previous dentist. This avoids repeating X-rays unnecessarily. Records usually arrive within two to three weeks, and your first appointment does not need to wait for them.

## Dental emergencies

We keep emergency appointment slots free every weekday. Telephone as early in the day as you can — slots are allocated in order of clinical need and they do get taken.

An emergency appointment costs £85 privately, or £27.90 as an NHS urgent course of treatment if you are an NHS patient here. This covers making you comfortable; any further treatment is arranged separately and charged separately.

Contact us the same day for:

- Severe toothache, particularly pain that keeps you awake
- Facial swelling, or swelling around a tooth
- A tooth knocked out or knocked loose by an injury
- Uncontrolled bleeding after an extraction
- A broken tooth causing pain or a sharp edge cutting your tongue or cheek
- A lost crown, filling or temporary filling causing pain

The following are not usually emergencies and can wait for a routine appointment: a lost filling or crown that is not painful, mild sensitivity, a chipped tooth with no pain, and routine check-ups.

## If a tooth is knocked out

For an adult permanent tooth, act quickly — the first hour matters most.

1. Pick the tooth up by the crown, not the root.
2. If it is dirty, rinse it briefly in milk or saline. Do not scrub it and do not use tap water for more than a second or two.
3. Try to put it back into the socket the right way round and bite gently on a clean handkerchief to hold it.
4. If you cannot reinsert it, keep it in a cup of milk. Do not let it dry out.
5. Telephone us immediately.

Do not attempt to reimplant a baby tooth, as it can damage the adult tooth developing underneath. Contact us for advice instead.

## When we are closed

Telephone the practice on 01632 960148. The answerphone message gives instructions for out-of-hours urgent dental care.

You can also call **NHS 111**, which operates 24 hours a day and can direct you to an urgent dental service.

Go to **A&E** if you have swelling that is closing your eye or affecting your breathing or swallowing, bleeding that will not stop, or an injury to your face or jaw. These need a hospital, not a dental practice.

## Registered with us but away from home

If you have a dental problem while travelling in the UK, any practice can see you as an emergency patient — you do not need to be registered with them. Search for an urgent dental service in the area, or call NHS 111. Keep the receipt and any notes, and let us know when you are back so we can update your records.
