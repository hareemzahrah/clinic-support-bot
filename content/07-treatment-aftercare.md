# Before and After Your Treatment

These are general instructions. Your dentist may give you different advice for your specific situation — where they differ, follow what your dentist told you.

## After a local anaesthetic

Your lip, tongue and cheek may stay numb for two to four hours. While numb:

- Do not eat anything that needs chewing. It is very easy to bite your lip or tongue without feeling it.
- Avoid hot drinks, as you will not feel a burn.
- Keep an eye on children, who are more likely to bite or chew numb areas.

If numbness lasts beyond about six hours, telephone the practice.

## After a filling

White fillings are set hard before you leave, so you can eat as soon as the numbness has gone.

Some sensitivity to hot, cold or biting pressure is normal for a few days and can last up to two weeks on a deep filling. Sensitivity that gets worse rather than better, or pain that wakes you at night, is not normal — contact us.

If the filling feels high when you bite together once the numbness has worn off, telephone us and we will adjust it. Do not wait — an unbalanced bite can cause pain and can crack the tooth.

## After an extraction

For the first 24 hours:

- Bite firmly on the gauze pad for 30 minutes. If bleeding continues, roll a clean handkerchief into a pad, bite on it for a further 30 minutes, and repeat if needed.
- Do not rinse your mouth at all. Rinsing washes away the blood clot that has to form for healing.
- Do not smoke. Smoking substantially increases the risk of a painful dry socket.
- Avoid alcohol, hot drinks and strenuous exercise.
- Eat soft food on the other side of your mouth.
- Do not poke the socket with your tongue or fingers.

From day two onward, rinse gently with warm salty water — half a teaspoon of salt in a mug of warm water — after meals and before bed, for about a week. Brush the rest of your mouth as normal, keeping the toothbrush away from the socket for the first few days.

Take over-the-counter painkillers as directed on the packet, if you can normally take them. Some discomfort and swelling for two to three days is expected.

**Contact us urgently if:** bleeding does not stop after an hour of pressure, pain becomes severe three to four days afterwards (a possible dry socket), you develop a fever, or swelling gets worse rather than better after day three.

## After root canal treatment

The tooth may be tender to bite on for several days, particularly if it was painful beforehand. Over-the-counter painkillers usually manage this.

Between appointments a temporary filling protects the tooth. Avoid chewing hard or sticky food on that side. If the temporary filling comes out, contact us promptly — the root canal system must not be left open.

A root-treated back tooth becomes brittle and usually needs a crown to protect it. Your dentist will discuss the timing with you. Delaying the crown risks the tooth fracturing beyond repair.

## Before and after a crown

Between your two appointments you will wear a temporary crown. It is deliberately less strongly cemented than the final one, so avoid chewing gum, toffee and other sticky foods on that side, and floss by pulling the floss out sideways rather than back up through the contact.

If a temporary crown comes off, keep it and telephone us. It can usually be recemented the same day.

After the final crown is fitted, mild gum tenderness for a few days is normal. As with a filling, tell us if the bite feels high.

## After a hygiene appointment

Gums may be tender and may bleed a little for a day or two, particularly if there was a lot of tartar or if you had gum disease. This settles. Keep brushing and cleaning between the teeth — stopping because it bleeds makes it worse, not better.

Teeth may be sensitive for a few days. A sensitive-teeth toothpaste, rubbed onto the sensitive area with a fingertip and left rather than rinsed off, usually helps.

## Home whitening

Wear the trays as instructed, either overnight or for the number of hours your dentist specified. Do not overfill the trays — a small dot of gel per tooth is enough, and excess gel on the gums causes irritation.

Sensitivity during whitening is common and temporary. If it becomes uncomfortable, skip a night or two rather than stopping altogether, and use a sensitive toothpaste alongside.

Avoid strongly coloured food and drink during the whitening period — red wine, coffee, tea, curry, tomato sauce, berries — and do not smoke.

Store gel in the fridge, not the freezer, and keep it away from children. Keep your trays; if you want to top up in a year or two you will only need to buy fresh gel.

Whitening does not change the colour of fillings, crowns, veneers or bridges. If you have these in visible positions, your dentist will discuss replacing them after whitening so that they match.
