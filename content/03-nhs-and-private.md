# NHS and Private Care

Ashfield Dental Practice provides both NHS and private treatment. This document explains the difference and which applies to you.

## Our NHS position

We hold an NHS contract, but it is limited and currently **fully allocated**. This means:

- Existing NHS patients at the practice continue to be seen under the NHS.
- Children under 18 of registered adult patients can be added to our NHS list.
- We are **not currently accepting new adult NHS patients**. We keep an expression-of-interest list and contact people when NHS capacity becomes available.
- Anyone can register as a private patient immediately.

If you need urgent dental care and are not registered with any practice, contact NHS 111, who can direct you to an urgent dental care service.

## NHS charges

NHS dental charges are set by the government, not by us. The same charge applies at every NHS practice in England. Current charges are:

| Band | Covers | Charge |
|---|---|---|
| Band 1 | Examination, diagnosis, advice, X-rays, scale and polish if clinically needed | £27.90 |
| Band 2 | Everything in Band 1 plus fillings, root canal treatment, extractions | £76.60 |
| Band 3 | Everything in Bands 1 and 2 plus crowns, bridges, dentures | £332.10 |
| Urgent | Urgent care such as pain relief or a temporary filling | £27.90 |

You pay one charge per course of treatment, not per item. If you need three fillings, you pay one Band 2 charge, not three.

If you are referred to another dentist for part of your treatment, you may pay a further charge to that practice.

## Free NHS treatment

You do not pay NHS dental charges if, at the time of your appointment, you are:

- Under 18
- Under 19 and in full-time education
- Pregnant, or have had a baby in the previous 12 months
- Staying in an NHS hospital and treated by the hospital dentist
- Receiving Income Support, Income-based Jobseeker's Allowance, Income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Pension Credit Guarantee Credit, or Universal Credit (subject to earnings thresholds)

You may also get help through the NHS Low Income Scheme. You will be asked to show evidence of your exemption. If you cannot provide it, you may be charged and asked to claim the money back, or you may receive a penalty notice.

## What the NHS does not cover

NHS dentistry provides treatment that is clinically necessary to keep your mouth, teeth and gums healthy. It does not cover treatment carried out purely for appearance. The following are private only, whichever list you are on:

- Teeth whitening
- Veneers for cosmetic reasons
- Composite bonding for cosmetic reasons
- Clear aligner orthodontics for adults
- White fillings on back teeth — the NHS provides amalgam on back teeth, and we will quote privately if you prefer white
- Hygienist appointments beyond a clinically necessary scale and polish

## Mixing NHS and private treatment

You can have NHS treatment and choose private treatment for specific items in the same course. Your dentist will make clear which items are NHS and which are private, and give you a written plan showing both before starting. You will never be moved from NHS to private treatment without your agreement.

## Choosing private care

Patients choose private treatment for longer appointment times, a wider choice of materials, appointments outside NHS availability, and access to treatments the NHS does not fund. Our private fees are in the Private Fee Guide.
